# Gameplay revision plan, November 20, 2026

Eight reviewers played through the whole game on paper: five took a slice of chapters each, and three looked across the whole game at onboarding, the puzzle difficulty curve, and mystery and tone. A planner merged their findings, and a critic checked the plan against the game files.

## How the game plays today

Today Night Nine opens well: the radio tuning, the identical 'good morning', the tape alignment and the overheard notebook call give a first-time player a strong hook, and the character beats (Iris choosing her name, Rook's identical take, Stephen's 'sixty-one percent', Nora's 'I am furious with you. I love you.') are the best thing in the game. The puzzle layer works against it. About 45 tasks are three-button choices that can be answered from the wording or brute-forced, because a wrong pick costs nothing and gets one generic sentence. Most case boards end with an order stage copied from the evidence card, and the board tells you how many placements fit, so three-piece stages can be solved by swapping. Key facts show up for the first time on the board that needs them (Admiral is a sea lion, the birthday photo times, Ward's bandage, Wick, Meridian), two chapters contradict earlier puzzles (Tomas's field dates, the Birch/Elm/Ash jacks), and big turns happen off screen (Steve's capture, the smashed rig, Saren at the tower, the Agnes reveal spoiled by a prompt). This revision keeps the premise, cast and chapter order. It makes wrong answers teach something and cost a click, shows the task goal on every board, plants each clue one scene before it is used, cuts copied order stages, fixes the continuity breaks, turns the emotional quiz questions into non-graded beats, and spends that difficulty on clue-driven puzzles (photo-strip alibi, the 'only the real Steve knows' test, the 'good stuff' slips, Ward's bandage, Mom's cut-up voicemail on the beach, the lullaby, Wick's page). Fear and thrill return in Chapters 3, 7, 8, 9 and 10 through short added lines. Voice cost: about 73 lines to record for priority 1, about 33 more for priority 2 and 8 for priority 3 (about 114 total against 338 existing; most are under 15 words). Moved lines keep their recorded audio: when a line moves to a new id, point its 'audio' field at the old file name. One new minor speaker (gate_guard) needs a voice in the ElevenLabs casting pass; everything else uses existing characters. Rebuttal text for wrong answers is on-screen text only and needs no recording.
